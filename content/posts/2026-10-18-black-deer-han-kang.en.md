After finishing this book, I was reminded of Shin Kyung-sook's *Violet*. And then I wondered to myself: why are works of Korean literature so devastating, painful, and unforgiving? Or is it just that the books I happen to read are particularly so? People facing the world day by day while carrying a deep sense of lack. A life that must somehow go on for the sole reason that one is still breathing—even if those wounds never heal for a lifetime, and even if unimaginable, horrific agony washes over them every time the memories surface. Every character depicted in the book carries trauma born from past events and experiences throughout their entire lives. Then, triggered by a certain moment, they step outside their daily routines; following unfamiliar environments and circumstances, they look back on the lives and relationships they have passed through, and in searching for themselves, they eventually come face to face with the pain they had been holding inside.

Why does our society—or our lives—need literature? I feel I can find the answer in the countless questions I asked myself while reading through this book. Heavy questions drifted through my mind, and perhaps because of that, I could not turn the pages easily and ended up reading it over the course of several weeks. Returning to reality, I am writing these words while once again asking myself those very questions. Above all, I hope that everyone can think of their own Uiseon somewhere out there, finding a pillar to lean on through each passing day.
